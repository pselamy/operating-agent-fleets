# Activity is not value: govern agents as software capabilities

If you operate persistent agents, this edition gives you a framework for deciding whether to invest, specialize, merge, repurpose, pause, or retire a capability without ranking unlike mandates or confusing activity with outcomes.

Persistent agents acquire names, voices, histories, and recognizable working styles. Personification can make an interface easier to use and remember. It can also obscure what the operator is governing: a deployed software capability with credentials, memory, schedules, dependencies, cost, consumers, and consequence.

“Which agent do I like?” is the wrong portfolio question. So is “Which agent sent the most messages?”

A quiet capability may deliver an outcome directly into another system. A talkative one may produce status traffic no one consumes. A specialist may look idle because its triggering work is rare but consequential. A generalist may look versatile because no one defined success.

Lifecycle terms here—invest, specialize, merge or subsume, repurpose, pause, and retire—describe software investment decisions. They are not employment judgments or moral claims about a personified interface.

## Begin with mandate thickness

“Software engineer,” “chief of staff,” or “help with real estate” names a theme, not an accountable capability.

A thick, versioned charter should define:

1. the bounded problem and decisions owned;
2. named consumers;
3. durable inputs and required evidence;
4. expected artifacts, actions, decisions, or state transitions;
5. read, proposal, mutation, transmission, and forbidden authority;
6. observable success conditions;
7. explicit non-goals; and
8. cost, cadence, privacy, credentials, and kill conditions.

That charter supplies the denominator for every later measure. Ten accepted outcomes may be excellent for a rare incident role and poor for a daily delivery role. Zero output may show underfeeding rather than model weakness. Repeated intervention may expose a vague authority boundary rather than poor reasoning.

A material change to mission, authority, consumers, outcomes, or non-goals needs an explicit decision bound to the proposed charter revision. Adding a subordinate capability should not silently redefine the primary mission.

## Use an evidence envelope, not a league table

Evaluate each capability within its own mandate using a common envelope:

- **Mandate thickness** — does the deployed configuration match the declared charter?
- **Consumed outcomes** — which named consumer actually used which immutable artifact, decision, or action?
- **Outcome quality** — correctness, timeliness, downstream acceptance, rework, escaped defects, and recurrence under pre-declared measures.
- **Steering burden** — corrections, restarts, clarifications, and manual recovery, separated from necessary human authority.
- **Operating burden** — model/tool cost, compute, storage, alerts, credentials, maintenance, and incident response.
- **Consequence-weighted risk** — sensitivity, authority, irreversibility, blast radius, custody, incident history, and required post-action evidence.
- **Distinctiveness and overlap** — what does another agent, worker pool, ordinary automation, or service already provide?
- **Learning velocity** — do failures become enforced tests, skills, policies, fixtures, or runbooks, and does recurrence decline?

Do not average those dimensions into one universal score. A scalar lets plentiful low-consequence activity offset one catastrophic high-consequence authority failure and hides the fact that mandates are unlike.

## Separate activity, availability, and consumed outcomes

Activity establishes that something emitted messages, commits, jobs, or status. Availability establishes that it responded or remained schedulable. Neither establishes that a named consumer received value.

A consumed outcome is a stronger starting point, but it still does not prove that the result was correct, cost-effective, safe, or repeatable. Preserve quality, burden, risk, and evidence context.

This separation also helps prevent an underfed capability from being mislabeled. No incoming bounded work says little about reasoning quality. Work with no named consumer points to a mandate or routing defect. Repeated consumer rejection is stronger evidence about quality or fit.

## Choose among six actions

- **Invest** when consumed outcomes, burden, risk, distinctiveness, and learning justify stronger tools, context, evaluation, or authority design. Preserve the charter, authority ceiling, baseline, and success gates so added resources cannot silently widen the mission.
- **Specialize** when a broad charter creates ambiguity but a narrower capability has clear consumers and unique value. Record which consumers, work, data, credentials, outcomes, and non-goals move to the specialist and what remains elsewhere.
- **Merge or subsume** when one accountable boundary can preserve outcomes with less routing and operating burden—while retaining predecessor lineage, consumers, work, safety constraints, credentials, data disposition, and rollback.
- **Repurpose** when the current mandate is weak but evidence supports a different bounded problem. Define the new charter before feeding work; do not rewrite the old history.
- **Pause** when value is uncertain, work is absent, risk is temporarily unacceptable, or a reversible probe can produce better evidence. Preserve identity, restoration steps, owner, and review deadline.
- **Retire** only after an authorized decision and verified cleanup of queued work, consumers, ownership, credentials, retained data, memory, resources, and the policy-defined period during which restoration remains possible.

These are proposed lifecycle actions, not claims that a private fleet already applies the protocol.

## Falsify suspected low value with a pause/feed probe

An impression is weaker than a reversible experiment.

Pre-register the exact charter, baseline, expected missing outcomes, safety exclusions, queue handling, restoration owner and objective, bounded backlog, named consumer, acceptance criteria, and abort conditions.

In the pause phase, narrow or stop the capability for a bounded window, record what breaks or routes elsewhere, abort on unexpected harm, then exercise and verify restoration. In the feed phase, provide defined work and measure consumed outcomes, quality, steering, cost, risk, distinctiveness, and learning.

This distinguishes “the capability cannot deliver” from “the system supplied no useful work.” Some safety, legal, money-or-asset custody, or irrecoverable-state capabilities must not be paused; the exclusion is evidence of criticality and non-pausability, not evidence of effectiveness or value.

## Human decisions must survive implementation

A signed lifecycle decision should preserve decision type, owner, charter revision, evidence window and limitations, deployed capability identity, consumer/work/data/memory/credential obligations, restore or reconstruction path, verification observer, postconditions, and reassessment trigger.

Humans retain authority to approve charter and authority changes, select lifecycle actions, authorize irreversible cleanup, own restoration, verify postconditions, and trigger reassessment. Agents may assemble evidence and propose actions; they do not acquire those decision rights from activity or recommendation confidence.

The action type matters. A specialization, merge, pause, and retirement carry different obligations. A generic “change ready” status loses them.

Assign a named reassessment owner and deadline. Reassess on schedule and when the charter, consumer, authority, incident history, overlap, operating burden, model, tools, or memory architecture changes materially. Reassessment returns the capability to observation; it does not permit silent retirement.

## What this edition does not claim

This public edition does not rank Patrick’s agents, publish private scorecards, claim a pause/feed result, infer value from message volume, or assert fleet-wide deployment of the protocol. Named consolidation, mandate, outcome, and charter-drift cases remain withheld until their restricted evidence and counterfactual limitations—including what might have happened under a different choice—pass the applicable disclosure review.

The public repository inventory establishes bounded repository counts and timestamps only. It does not establish usefulness, originality, adoption, maintained quality, or agent contribution.
